Title: What Is Gender Ideology?
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: July 7, 2023
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/commentary/what-gender-ideology

Richards received inquiries from reporters working on stories about the term "gender ideology" and set out to define it clearly. He argues that gender ideology is the source of the belief that children can be born in the wrong body -- a claim that has gained widespread acceptance despite its radical departure from biological reality.

One cannot really define gender ideology without invoking some of the terms it has already inserted into our language. The very vocabulary we use -- terms like "gender identity," "assigned sex at birth," and "transgender" -- presupposes the framework that gender ideology promotes. This linguistic infiltration makes it difficult to critique the ideology without inadvertently reinforcing its assumptions.

Gender ideology does not accommodate the reality of sex -- the reproductive strategy of mammals including human beings. Sex is a biological fact rooted in the organization of bodies toward reproduction. Males produce small gametes (sperm), and females produce large gametes (ova). This binary is not a social construct or a spectrum but a fundamental feature of mammalian biology.

At its core, gender ideology holds that every person has an inner sense of gender -- a "gender identity" -- that may or may not align with their biological sex. When there is a mismatch, the ideology insists that the inner sense of gender should take precedence over the body. This leads to the conclusion that medical and social interventions should alter the body and social reality to match the person's felt identity, rather than helping the person accept their biological reality.

Richards argues that this ideology has profound implications for law, medicine, education, and culture. It undermines sex-based protections for women, confuses children about basic biological realities, and leads to irreversible medical interventions on minors who may simply be going through normal developmental confusion. Understanding what gender ideology is -- and naming it clearly -- is the first step toward countering its influence on our institutions.

Title: Why States Must Define Sex Precisely
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: March 2023
Source: The Heritage Foundation / Public Discourse
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/commentary/why-states-must-define-sex-precisely

Until recently, no precise legal definition of sex was needed because no one contested it. The biological distinction between male and female was so obvious and universally understood that lawmakers never felt compelled to spell it out in statute. Because of gender ideology's growing influence on laws and institutions, states no longer have this luxury.

Current efforts to redefine sex to include "gender identity" would dissolve sex as a stable legal category and create legal chaos. If sex is reduced to a subjective feeling rather than an objective biological reality, then every law, regulation, and policy built on the distinction between male and female becomes incoherent. Title IX protections for women in education, sex-segregated prisons, women's shelters, and sports categories all depend on a clear and stable definition of sex.

Any good definition will capture the central concept of biological sex -- the orientation of male and female bodies for reproduction. Males are organized around the production of small gametes (sperm), and females are organized around the production of large gametes (ova). This is a binary classification rooted in reproductive biology, not a spectrum or a social construct.

A sound legal definition will also refer to what happens under normal development while accounting for disorders. Disorders of sexual development (sometimes called "intersex" conditions) do not create a third sex or disprove the sex binary. They are medical conditions that affect the normal development of male or female bodies, just as other developmental disorders affect other bodily systems without creating new categories of human beings.

Public institutions must shore up their defenses by defining sex precisely in law. Without clear definitions, courts and bureaucrats will continue to reinterpret existing law through the lens of gender ideology, eroding sex-based protections and creating confusion. States that act now to define sex clearly in their legal codes will be better positioned to protect women's rights, children's welfare, and the integrity of their institutions against ideological capture.

Title: San Francisco's Perverse Incentive to Identify as Transgender
Authors: Jay W. Richards and Jamie Hall
Date: November 2022
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/commentary/san-franciscos-perverse-incentive-identify-transgender

San Francisco created the Guaranteed Income for Transgender People (GIFT) program, providing 55 eligible residents with $1,200 per month for 18 months, as well as health care and financial coaching. While framed as a compassionate measure to help a marginalized population, the program raises serious questions about incentive structures and the integrity of gender identity claims.

The authors question whether, if this program expands, the number of San Francisco's poor who say they identify as transgender will also expand. This is not an idle concern. Gender identity, as currently understood in law and policy, is entirely self-declared. There is no objective test, no medical diagnosis required, and no verification process. Anyone can claim a transgender identity, and institutions are expected to accept that claim without question.

When a government program offers substantial financial benefits -- $1,200 per month plus health care and financial coaching -- exclusively to people who identify as transgender, it creates a direct financial incentive for people to adopt that identity. This is especially true among the poor, for whom $1,200 per month represents a significant sum. The program essentially pays people to claim a transgender identity, with no way to distinguish genuine cases from opportunistic ones.

This is a textbook example of a perverse incentive -- a policy that produces unintended consequences by rewarding behavior the policymakers did not intend to encourage. Economists have long understood that when you subsidize something, you get more of it. If San Francisco subsidizes transgender identification, it should expect more people to identify as transgender, regardless of whether their felt sense of identity has actually changed.

The broader implications are troubling. If gender identity is truly innate and immutable, as activists claim, then financial incentives should have no effect on the number of people who identify as transgender. If the numbers do increase in response to financial incentives, it would suggest that transgender identification is, at least in some cases, influenced by external factors rather than being a fixed internal state. Either way, the GIFT program represents a troubling experiment in social engineering.

Title: Florida's Parental Rights in Education Bill Hits Target: Gender Ideology Harms Kids
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: 2022
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/education/commentary/floridas-parental-rights-education-bill-hits-target-gender-ideology-harms-kids

Florida's Parental Rights in Education bill became the subject of intense national debate, with opponents branding it the "Don't Say Gay" bill. But this characterization is deeply misleading. The bill would not ban the word "gay" but rather protect children, require schools to be transparent with parents regarding health services, and prohibit elementary school teachers from pushing classroom discussion of sexual orientation and gender identity on young children.

The core provision of the bill is straightforward: classroom instruction on sexual orientation and gender identity is not appropriate for children in kindergarten through third grade. These are children aged five through eight -- an age at which discussions of complex sexual and gender topics are developmentally inappropriate and potentially harmful. The bill does not prevent older students from learning about these topics, nor does it prohibit any student from talking about their family or their own experiences.

The bill also includes important transparency provisions. Schools would be required to inform parents about health services offered to their children and to obtain parental consent before providing such services. This is a basic principle of parental rights -- parents have the right to know what is happening with their children in school, especially when it comes to sensitive matters of health and identity.

Gender ideology poses a genuine threat to children's well-being. Research shows that the vast majority of children who experience gender dysphoria will naturally resolve it through puberty if not subjected to social or medical interventions. Teaching young children that they might be "born in the wrong body" or that their gender identity might not match their sex can create confusion and distress where none previously existed. Florida's bill recognizes this reality and takes a common-sense step to protect young children from ideological content they are not equipped to process.

The hysteria surrounding the bill reveals more about its opponents than about the legislation itself. Protecting young children from inappropriate content is not bigotry -- it is responsible governance.

Title: What Is Zionism? What Is Christian Zionism?
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: January 2026
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/antisemitism/commentary/what-zionism-what-christian-zionism

Basic decency provides more than enough grounds for Christians to oppose hateful and irrational attacks against Jews and Israel. We should treat these episodes as tests of our courage and discernment, Richards argues, rather than occasions for moral equivocation or political calculation.

The term "Zionism" has become one of the most contested words in contemporary political discourse, but much of the debate suffers from a lack of clarity about what the term actually means. Some people understand "Zionism" in its original sense, connected to Theodore Herzl's 19th-century movement for a Jewish homeland. That political movement, which emerged in response to persistent European antisemitism, advocated for the establishment of a sovereign Jewish state in the historical land of Israel.

But that political movement was not the same as broader theological hopes that long preceded it. Jewish longing for a return to the land of Israel stretches back millennia, rooted in biblical promises and sustained through centuries of exile and persecution. Herzl's political Zionism channeled these ancient hopes into a concrete political program, but the two should not be conflated.

Richards describes five types of Protestant "Apocalyptic Zionism" -- various theological frameworks through which Protestant Christians have understood the relationship between the Jewish people, the land of Israel, and biblical prophecy. These range from dispensationalist views that see the modern state of Israel as a direct fulfillment of end-times prophecy to more moderate positions that affirm Jewish connection to the land without tying it to a specific prophetic timeline.

Understanding these distinctions matters because critics of Israel often use "Zionism" as a catch-all term of condemnation, lumping together diverse religious, political, and cultural positions under a single pejorative label. This conflation makes productive dialogue nearly impossible and often serves as a thin cover for antisemitism.

Christians need not adopt any particular prophetic framework to recognize that the Jewish people have a legitimate historical and moral claim to a homeland, and that the hatred directed at Jews and Israel today deserves clear and unequivocal opposition.

Title: Perfect Eclipses: Coincidence or Conspiracy?
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: September 2017
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/perfect-eclipses-coincidence-or-conspiracy/

A rare alignment allows Earthlings to witness "perfect" solar eclipses -- our Moon just barely covers the Sun's bright photosphere, revealing the otherwise invisible solar corona in a spectacular display. This precise match depends on just the right sizes, shapes, and relative distances of the Sun, Moon, and Earth, with no law of physics dictating this layout.

The Sun is approximately 400 times larger than the Moon, but it is also approximately 400 times farther away. This remarkable coincidence means that the two bodies appear almost exactly the same size in our sky. During a total solar eclipse, the Moon's disk covers the Sun's bright surface almost perfectly, leaving just the wispy corona visible around the edges. This is not something that had to be the case -- it is a contingent arrangement that could easily have been otherwise.

There are 65 major moons in our Solar System, but only we enjoy perfect solar eclipses. Other planets with moons do not have this precise size-distance relationship. Jupiter's moons, for instance, are either too large or too small relative to the Sun as seen from Jupiter's surface to produce the same effect. Earth's perfect eclipses are, as far as we know, unique in the Solar System.

Our ability to observe perfect solar eclipses has played a key role in several major scientific discoveries. The solar corona, which is only visible during total eclipses, has provided crucial data about the Sun's atmosphere and magnetic field. Arthur Eddington's observations during the 1919 total solar eclipse provided the first empirical confirmation of Einstein's general theory of relativity, by measuring the bending of starlight around the Sun. The element helium was first discovered in the solar spectrum during an eclipse.

Richards argues that this convergence of factors -- the precise arrangement that produces perfect eclipses on the one planet in the Solar System that also happens to have observers capable of appreciating and learning from them -- points beyond mere coincidence toward purpose and design in the cosmos.

Title: The Road Map for Winning the Culture War Through Faith, Reason
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: May 30, 2024
Source: The Daily Signal
URL: https://www.dailysignal.com/2024/05/30/how-faith-reason-can-win-culture-war/

Jay Richards discusses the roadmap for winning the culture war through an alliance of faith and reason, drawing on themes from his book co-authored with James Robison, "Fight the Good Fight: How an Alliance of Faith and Reason Can Win the Culture War."

The culture war is not merely a political conflict — it is fundamentally a spiritual and intellectual battle. The forces arrayed against traditional values, religious liberty, and the natural family are motivated by ideologies that have their roots in bad philosophy: materialism, relativism, and a radical rejection of the created order.

To win this battle, conservatives must combine the best of faith and reason. Faith without reason becomes fideism — a blind belief disconnected from reality. Reason without faith becomes a truncated rationalism incapable of grounding human dignity, natural rights, or moral truth.

The good news is that faith and reason are natural allies, not enemies. The Christian intellectual tradition has always held that God created an intelligible universe and gave human beings the rational capacity to understand it. This is why the Christian West gave rise to universities, hospitals, and modern science itself.

The practical implications of this alliance are significant. On issues like gender ideology, the case against radical interventions on children's bodies is both scientific (the evidence for the "affirm only" model is weak) and moral (children cannot consent to irreversible procedures). On economic questions, both economic science and moral theology support the dignity of work and the importance of free exchange. On questions of life and family, reason and revelation speak with one voice.

The path forward requires courage, clarity, and the willingness to speak truth in a culture that increasingly punishes dissent. But the resources of the Christian intellectual tradition are more than adequate for the task.

Title: Will Left's Gender Agenda Fall Flat With Minorities and Working Americans?
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: 2022
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/commentary/will-lefts-gender-agenda-fall-flat-minorities-and-working-americans

A new, glossy messaging document reveals the plans of left-wing groups to sell American voters on gender theory, produced jointly by the Transgender Law Center, ASO Communications, and Lake Research Partners.

The document represents a sophisticated public relations campaign designed to make gender ideology palatable to demographic groups that have traditionally been skeptical of it — including racial minorities and working-class Americans. The messaging playbook acknowledges, implicitly, that gender ideology does not enjoy broad public support and requires careful framing to avoid triggering the common-sense objections that most people naturally have.

The messaging strategy relies on emotional appeals and vague language about "freedom" and "acceptance" while carefully avoiding the specific policy implications that would alarm most parents and voters. The document coaches activists to talk about "letting people be who they are" rather than discussing the concrete reality of medical interventions on minors, male-bodied individuals in women's spaces, or the erasure of sex-based legal protections.

This approach is telling. If gender ideology were truly self-evident and supported by robust evidence, its advocates would not need a sophisticated messaging campaign to sell it to the public. The very existence of this document is an admission that the ideology fails on its own terms and must be wrapped in the language of freedom and compassion to gain political traction.

The question is whether minorities and working Americans will buy what the activists are selling. There are reasons for skepticism. These communities tend to be more religious, more traditional in their views of family and gender, and less susceptible to the academic jargon that surrounds gender theory in elite institutions.
