Title: Democrats Fixate On Gay Marriage While The Country Crumbles
Authors: Jay W. Richards and Jared Eckert
Date: August 2022
Source: The Federalist
URL: https://thefederalist.com/2022/08/01/democrats-fixate-on-gay-marriage-while-the-country-crumbles/

Co-authored with Jared Eckert, this article critiques the Democratic Party's push for the Respect for Marriage Act at a time when the country faced pressing economic and social crises. The authors argue that the legislation was a political distraction designed to energize the Democratic base and put Republicans in an uncomfortable position rather than a response to any genuine urgent need.

The Respect for Marriage Act would codify same-sex marriage in federal law, ostensibly to protect against a potential Supreme Court reversal of the Obergefell decision. Richards and Eckert argue that this scenario was far-fetched and that the legislation's real purpose was political rather than practical.

Meanwhile, the country faced skyrocketing inflation, rising crime, an ongoing border crisis, supply chain disruptions, and declining public trust in institutions. The authors contend that the Democratic leadership's decision to prioritize symbolic legislation on same-sex marriage -- an issue already settled by Supreme Court precedent -- while ignoring these urgent problems reveals misplaced priorities.

The article also raises concerns about the Respect for Marriage Act's implications for religious liberty. The authors argue that the legislation lacked adequate protections for religious organizations, businesses, and individuals who hold traditional views on marriage. Without robust conscience protections, the Act could be used as a weapon against religious institutions that maintain traditional marriage teachings -- forcing them to choose between their convictions and their ability to operate in the public square.

Richards and Eckert call on lawmakers to focus on the issues that matter most to the American people and to ensure that any marriage legislation includes meaningful protections for religious liberty.
