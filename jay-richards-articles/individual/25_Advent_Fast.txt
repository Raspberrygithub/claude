Title: We've (Mostly) Abandoned the Advent Fast. But It's Not Too Late to Start
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: Undated
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/weve-mostly-abandoned-the-advent-fast-but-its-not-too-late-to-start/

Advent, like Lent, was traditionally a time of repentance and preparation. Just as Lent prepares us for the great feast of Easter, so Advent prepares us for the great feast of Christmas. Yet while Lent has retained at least a vestige of its penitential character in many Christian traditions, Advent has been almost entirely overtaken by the secular Christmas season.

Richards laments that for most Christians today, Advent is indistinguishable from the holiday festivities that surround it. Instead of a season of fasting, prayer, and spiritual preparation, it has become a season of parties, shopping, and indulgence. The result is that Christmas itself loses much of its power -- when there is no fast, the feast feels less like a feast.

The traditional Advent fast, practiced in various forms across Christian history, involved abstaining from certain foods, increasing prayer, and engaging in acts of charity and repentance. This was not mere legalism but a recognition that the coming of Christ into the world is an event of such magnitude that it deserves genuine spiritual preparation.

Richards argues that recovering the Advent fast would enrich the Christian experience of Christmas immeasurably. The contrast between the discipline of Advent and the joy of Christmas Day creates a rhythm of anticipation and fulfillment that mirrors the deeper spiritual reality of waiting for Christ. Without the preparation, the celebration is diminished.

The article offers practical suggestions for Christians who wish to adopt an Advent fast, even if their particular tradition does not currently observe one. Richards encourages readers to start where they are -- whether that means fasting from certain foods, reducing entertainment, increasing prayer time, or simply creating space for quiet reflection during a season that the surrounding culture fills with noise and activity.
