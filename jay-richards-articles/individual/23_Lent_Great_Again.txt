Title: Let's Make Lent Great Again, Together
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: 2019
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/lets-make-lent-great-together/

Lent culminates with Easter, the great feast of the resurrection -- the central event of the Christian faith. Unlike the great feast of Christmas, which the secular world has enthusiastically adopted (if in commercialized form), the secular world mostly ignores Easter. And the world passes over in silence the long season of fasting and penance that precedes Easter.

Richards reflects on the meaning and practice of Lent, the 40-day period of prayer, fasting, and repentance that prepares Christians for the celebration of Christ's resurrection. He argues that Lent has been diminished in much of contemporary Christianity, reduced to a token gesture of "giving something up" rather than embraced as the serious spiritual discipline it was meant to be.

The traditional Lenten fast was rigorous -- not merely abstaining from chocolate or social media, but genuine fasting that involved real hunger and real sacrifice. This kind of fasting, Richards argues, has both spiritual and physical benefits. It disciplines the body, clarifies the mind, and draws the believer into closer communion with God.

Richards calls on Christians to recover the fullness of the Lenten tradition. This means embracing fasting not as a mere dietary modification but as a spiritual practice rooted in Scripture and sustained by centuries of Christian wisdom. It means taking prayer and repentance seriously during Lent, using the season as a genuine preparation for the joy of Easter rather than a perfunctory religious obligation.

The article also touches on the communal dimension of Lent. Fasting together, as a community of believers, strengthens the bonds of fellowship and mutual encouragement. Richards suggests that recovering Lent as a shared practice -- not just an individual one -- could help revitalize Christian communities that have lost touch with the richness of their liturgical tradition.
