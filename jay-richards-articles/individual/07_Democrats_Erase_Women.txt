Title: Democrats Erase Women Through Budget 'Reconciliation'
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: October 2021
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/commentary/democrats-erase-women-through-budget-reconciliation

The $3.5 trillion tax-and-spend bill offered an opportunity for gender activists to incorporate ideological language into federal legislation, and they seized it. Buried within the massive reconciliation bill are linguistic choices that reveal the growing influence of gender ideology on the Democratic Party and its legislative agenda.

The "Maternal Mortality" section (Part 4 of Subtitle J of Title III) consists of 15 sections appropriating funds related to women's health, yet uses gender-neutral terminology -- "pregnant, lactating, and postpartum individuals" -- repeated 18 times throughout the text. The irony is striking: a section ostensibly devoted to maternal health cannot bring itself to use the word "mother" or even "woman."

"Aren't Democrats supposed to be the party of feminists?" Richards asks. For decades, the Democratic Party positioned itself as the champion of women's rights. Yet here it is, systematically erasing the very concept of womanhood from legislation designed to protect women's health. The term "pregnant individuals" is not a neutral or inclusive improvement -- it is an ideological statement that denies the fundamental connection between pregnancy and womanhood.

Only women can become pregnant, lactate, and experience postpartum conditions. This is not a controversial claim -- it is a basic biological fact. By replacing "women" and "mothers" with "individuals," the legislation implicitly endorses the claim that men can become pregnant, that biological sex is irrelevant to reproduction, and that acknowledging the female body's unique role in reproduction is somehow exclusionary.

This linguistic erasure has real consequences. When legislation cannot name the population it is designed to serve, it becomes harder to study, track, and address the specific health needs of that population. Women's health research depends on the ability to identify and study women as a distinct biological category. Gender-neutral language obscures this category and undermines the very goals the legislation claims to advance.

The insertion of gender ideology into budget reconciliation demonstrates how deeply this ideology has penetrated the Democratic Party's legislative apparatus. It is no longer confined to cultural debates -- it is being written into the laws that govern federal spending and health policy.
