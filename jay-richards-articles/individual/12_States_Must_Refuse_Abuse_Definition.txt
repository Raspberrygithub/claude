Title: States Must Refuse to Define 'Abuse' as Raising a Child According to His or Her Sex
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: April 2025
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/report/states-must-refuse-define-abuse-raising-child-according-his-or-her-sex

This Heritage Foundation report addresses the alarming trend in some jurisdictions of treating parents' refusal to affirm a child's claimed transgender identity as a form of child abuse. Richards argues that states must firmly reject this dangerous redefinition.

The report examines how gender ideology has influenced child welfare definitions and policies. In some states and localities, parents who decline to use a child's preferred pronouns, who refuse to consent to puberty blockers or cross-sex hormones, or who simply raise their children in accordance with their biological sex risk being reported to child protective services and investigated for abuse or neglect.

This represents a radical inversion of the concept of child abuse. Traditionally, child abuse involves actions that cause physical or psychological harm to a child. Under the new framework promoted by gender activists, it is the refusal to facilitate potentially harmful medical interventions -- puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones, and surgeries -- that constitutes abuse. Parents who take a cautious, evidence-based approach to their child's gender confusion are treated as threats rather than protectors.

Richards argues that states must proactively define their child welfare laws to prevent this ideological capture. Legislation should make clear that raising a child in accordance with his or her biological sex is not abuse, that parents have the right to seek therapeutic approaches that do not involve medical transition, and that disagreement with gender ideology does not constitute grounds for state intervention in the family.

The report provides model language and policy recommendations for state legislators seeking to protect parental rights against the encroachment of gender ideology into child welfare systems.
