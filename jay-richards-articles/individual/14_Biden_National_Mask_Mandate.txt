Title: Biden's National Mask Mandate is Absurd and Despotic
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: 2020
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/bidens-national-mask-mandate-is-absurd-and-despotic/

Richards argues that the Biden-Harris ticket is "off to a despotic start" with Biden's call for a nationwide mask mandate. The proposal, Richards contends, reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of both the science of masking and the proper limits of presidential authority.

Local mask mandates often require cloth coverings rather than surgical or N95 masks. Nearly anything covering the face qualifies as a "mask" -- bandanas, scarves, and homemade cloth coverings that provide minimal, if any, protection against viral transmission. The gap between what people think of when they hear "mask mandate" and what such mandates actually require is enormous.

There are "so many ifs involved with masks that it's absurd to mandate them for everyone," Richards writes. He contrasts the case of a healthcare worker in a nursing home -- where masking with proper equipment in a high-risk setting makes clear sense -- with that of a lone young golfer outdoors, where the risk of transmission is negligible and a mask serves no practical purpose. A one-size-fits-all mandate cannot account for the vast differences in risk across settings, activities, and populations.

No presidential candidate should suggest he has the competence, or authority, to dictate such choices for everyone. The decision about when and where to wear a mask involves countless variables that differ from person to person and situation to situation. A national mandate treats a complex, context-dependent question as if it has a single, simple answer that can be imposed from Washington.

Richards argues that the impulse toward national mandates reflects a broader tendency to centralize decisions that are better left to individuals, families, and local communities. Even if masking were unambiguously beneficial in all situations -- which it is not -- the power to mandate it nationally would represent an unprecedented expansion of executive authority with troubling implications for individual liberty.
