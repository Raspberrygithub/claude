Title: Rikers Rape Case Shows Female Prisoners Are the Voiceless Victims of Gender Ideology
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: January 2024
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/commentary/rikers-rape-case-shows-female-prisoners-are-the-voiceless-victims-gender-ideology

A lawsuit was filed against the New York Department of Correction that lays bare the dangers of housing male inmates in women's prisons based solely on their claimed gender identity. The anonymous plaintiff, Rose Doe, says she was groped and raped by a male prisoner in 2022 after that prisoner was transferred to a women's facility.

The suit alleges the perpetrator told a prison mate he claimed to be "trans" just so he could gain access to women. Even more disturbing, a correction officer even allegedly coached the defendant on how to get transferred to the women's facility. This case illustrates a fundamental problem with gender identity policies in prisons: they create an obvious pathway for predatory men to gain access to vulnerable women.

There is no blood test, brain scan, or infallible battery of questions that could allow a prison doctor to separate the trans wheat from the cheater chaff. If a man says he identifies as a woman, that's enough. The system is built entirely on self-declaration, with no objective verification possible. This makes it inherently exploitable by any male inmate who wishes to be housed with women, regardless of his true motivations.

Female prisoners are among the most vulnerable people in our society. Many have histories of abuse and trauma. Placing biological males in their housing units -- regardless of how those males identify -- exposes these women to serious risk of sexual assault and psychological harm. Yet the women have little recourse. They cannot leave. They cannot choose their housing. They are, in the most literal sense, a captive population subjected to policies imposed by ideologues who will never face the consequences.

For far too long, we have allowed ourselves to be gaslighted into conceding what we all know: that biological sex is real, that it matters, and that policies ignoring it put women at risk. The Rikers case should serve as a wake-up call. Gender identity policies in prisons are not compassionate -- they are dangerous, and the women who suffer the consequences deserve to be heard.
