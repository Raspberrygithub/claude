Title: Why Is Congress Facilitating Prison Rape in the Name of Fairness?
Author: Jay W. Richards and Sarah Parshall Perry
Date: September 25, 2024
Source: Washington Times
URL: https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2024/sep/25/why-is-congress-facilitating-prison-rape-in-name-o/

Prison rape is horrific in any scenario, but gender ideologues in Washington have found a way to make it more common — all in the name of fairness to men who "identify as women."

In 2020, Tomieika Johnson, who had been in an abusive marriage before her incarceration, was forced to live with a male serial rapist, Richard Masbruch, at the Central California Women's Facility. When she raised concerns about increasing incidents including rape, she was told to "give it a chance."

More than 20 years ago, President George W. Bush signed the Prison Rape Elimination Act (PREA) into law, with the goal of eradicating prisoner rape. A subcommittee of the Senate Judiciary Committee, chaired by Cory Booker, held a hearing to assess the state of sexual assault since the law passed.

However, the hearing largely avoided the precipitous rise in sexual assault of women prisoners forced to share confined spaces with men identifying as women.

Women should not be sacrificed on the altar of gender ideology just because they're behind bars. The fact that incarcerated women are among the most vulnerable populations in our society — many having experienced domestic violence, sexual assault, and trafficking before their incarceration — makes the placement of biological males in their living spaces especially cruel.

The policy of housing biological males with female inmates based solely on the male's declared gender identity creates predictable and preventable harm. No amount of ideological conviction can change the basic reality that placing male-bodied individuals in confined spaces with vulnerable women increases the risk of sexual violence.
