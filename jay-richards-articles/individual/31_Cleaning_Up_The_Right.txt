Title: Cleaning Up the Right
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: Undated
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/cleaning-up-the-right/

Richards addresses the need for the conservative movement to police its own ranks and maintain intellectual and moral standards. The article argues that the right must be willing to confront and distance itself from bad actors, bad arguments, and bad faith within its own coalition.

The conservative movement, like any broad political coalition, attracts a range of voices -- some thoughtful and principled, others opportunistic or extreme. Richards contends that the movement's credibility and effectiveness depend on its willingness to distinguish between legitimate conservative ideas and the distortions, conspiracy theories, and bigotry that sometimes attach themselves to the right.

This does not mean adopting the left's standards for what counts as acceptable discourse. The left has a well-documented tendency to smear mainstream conservative positions as extremist, racist, or hateful. Conservatives should not internalize these bad-faith attacks or allow their opponents to define the boundaries of acceptable thought.

But neither should conservatives refuse all self-examination or circle the wagons around every person and idea that claims the conservative label. A movement that cannot distinguish between principled conservatism and its counterfeit versions will ultimately discredit itself. Richards argues for a conservatism that is confident enough in its principles to enforce standards without being paranoid about internal criticism.

The article offers guidance on how to navigate this balance -- how to identify genuine problems within the right without falling into the trap of accepting the left's framing, and how to maintain intellectual rigor and moral seriousness in a political environment that rewards outrage and tribal loyalty over careful thought.
