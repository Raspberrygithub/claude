Title: How To Mount a Religious Liberty Challenge to a Childhood Vaccine Mandate
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: August 2025
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/parental-rights/commentary/how-mount-religious-liberty-challenge-childhood-vaccine-mandate

Richards argues that parents can have legitimate reasons to delay or decline vaccines on the CDC's childhood schedule, and that religious liberty protections should apply to these decisions. The article provides guidance for parents seeking to challenge vaccine mandates on religious grounds.

The piece acknowledges that vaccines have played an important role in public health but contends that this does not justify overriding parental rights or religious convictions. Parents who object to certain vaccines -- whether because of concerns about the use of fetal cell lines in vaccine development, the specific risks associated with particular vaccines, or broader convictions about bodily autonomy rooted in religious belief -- deserve legal protection.

Richards outlines the legal framework for mounting a religious liberty challenge to childhood vaccine mandates. This includes the First Amendment's Free Exercise Clause, the Religious Freedom Restoration Act (RFRA) where applicable, and state-level religious exemption provisions. He discusses the standards courts have applied in evaluating religious exemption claims and offers practical advice for parents navigating the process.

The article also addresses the broader tension between public health authority and individual liberty. While governments have a legitimate interest in preventing the spread of infectious disease, this interest must be balanced against fundamental rights of parents to make medical decisions for their children and to raise them according to their religious convictions.

Richards expresses hope that the Supreme Court will recognize those rights if called upon to adjudicate a case involving childhood vaccine mandates. He argues that the Court's recent jurisprudence on religious liberty -- including decisions strengthening free exercise protections -- provides a strong foundation for protecting parental rights in this context.
