Title: What is Zionism? What is Christian Zionism?
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: January 2026
Source: Public Discourse
URL: https://www.thepublicdiscourse.com/2026/01/99905/

This Public Discourse version of Richards's article on Zionism and Christian Zionism explores the same themes as the Heritage Foundation version but is tailored for the academic and intellectually engaged audience of the Witherspoon Institute's journal.

Richards provides a careful examination of the term "Zionism" and its various meanings in historical, political, and theological context. He traces the term from Theodore Herzl's 19th-century political movement for a Jewish homeland to its contemporary usage in political discourse, where it has become a term of both pride and condemnation depending on the speaker.

The article distinguishes between political Zionism as a specific historical movement and the broader theological and cultural traditions of Jewish connection to the land of Israel. These are related but distinct phenomena, and conflating them leads to confusion and misunderstanding.

Richards devotes particular attention to Christian Zionism, examining its various forms within Protestant theology. He identifies multiple strands of Christian thought about the relationship between the Jewish people, the land of Israel, and biblical prophecy, ranging from dispensationalist frameworks that see modern Israel as a direct fulfillment of end-times prophecy to more moderate theological positions.

The article argues that Christians need not adopt any particular eschatological framework to recognize the legitimacy of Jewish connection to the land of Israel or to oppose antisemitism. Basic decency and a commitment to truth are sufficient grounds for Christians to stand against the hateful and irrational attacks on Jews and Israel that have proliferated in recent years.

Richards calls for clarity of thought and courage of conviction in engaging with these contested questions, treating them as tests of intellectual honesty and moral seriousness.
