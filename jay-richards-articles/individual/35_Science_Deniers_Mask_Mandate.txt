Title: Only Science-Deniers Believe In A National Mask Mandate
Authors: Jay W. Richards, Douglas Axe, and William Briggs
Date: November 2020
Source: The Federalist
URL: https://thefederalist.com/2020/11/02/only-science-deniers-believe-in-a-national-mask-mandate/

Co-authored with Douglas Axe and William Briggs, this article challenges the scientific basis for a national mask mandate. The provocative title deliberately inverts the common accusation that mask skeptics are "science deniers," arguing instead that the evidence does not support universal masking requirements imposed at the national level.

The authors examine the available scientific evidence on mask effectiveness, distinguishing between different types of masks and different settings. They argue that the evidence for universal cloth masking as a significant factor in reducing community transmission of COVID-19 is far weaker than proponents suggest. Randomized controlled trials -- the gold standard of medical evidence -- have produced mixed results at best.

The article draws a distinction between targeted, evidence-based masking recommendations (such as N-95 masks for healthcare workers in clinical settings) and blanket mandates requiring the general public to wear cloth coverings in all public spaces. The former has a reasonable scientific basis; the latter, the authors argue, does not.

Richards, Axe, and Briggs contend that the push for a national mask mandate is driven more by politics than by science. The mandate has become a symbol of compliance and social solidarity rather than a carefully calibrated public health measure. Those who question it are labeled "science deniers" not because the science clearly supports mandates but because questioning the mandate has become socially and politically unacceptable.

The authors call for a return to evidence-based public health policy, in which recommendations are proportionate to the evidence, targeted to the settings where they are most likely to be effective, and respectful of individual liberty and judgment. A one-size-fits-all national mandate, they argue, fails on all three counts.
