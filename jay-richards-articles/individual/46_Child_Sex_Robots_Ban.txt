Title: Congress's Ban on Child Sex Robots: It's a Start
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: Undated
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/congresss-ban-child-sex-robots-start/

Richards addresses congressional legislation banning child sex robots and argues that while the ban is a welcome and necessary step, it is only a start in confronting the broader technological and moral challenges posed by the intersection of artificial intelligence, robotics, and human sexuality.

The existence of child sex robots represents a deeply disturbing development in the commercialization of technology. These devices are designed to simulate sexual interactions with children, and their manufacturers have argued that they serve as harmless outlets that might even reduce offending against real children. Richards rejects this argument, contending that such devices normalize the sexual exploitation of children rather than preventing it.

The article examines the psychological and moral arguments against child sex robots. Far from serving as a "safe" alternative to actual abuse, these devices reinforce and strengthen the desires and behavioral patterns that lead to child sexual abuse. They treat children -- even in simulated form -- as objects of sexual gratification, and they desensitize users to the boundary between fantasy and reality.

Richards argues that the congressional ban reflects a correct moral intuition: some things are so inherently degrading that they should not be permitted regardless of whether they cause direct, measurable harm to identifiable victims. The ban recognizes that a society's moral character is shaped by what it tolerates, and that tolerating child sex robots would represent a profound moral failure.

However, the ban is only a start. Richards calls for broader vigilance regarding the ways in which emerging technologies can be used to exploit the vulnerable, and for a willingness to impose moral limits on technology when those technologies threaten human dignity and the protection of children.
