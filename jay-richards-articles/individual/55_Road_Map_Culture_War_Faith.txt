Title: The Road Map for Winning the Culture War Through Faith, Reason
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: May 30, 2024
Source: The Daily Signal
URL: https://www.dailysignal.com/2024/05/30/how-faith-reason-can-win-culture-war/

Jay Richards discusses the roadmap for winning the culture war through an alliance of faith and reason, drawing on themes from his book co-authored with James Robison, "Fight the Good Fight: How an Alliance of Faith and Reason Can Win the Culture War."

The culture war is not merely a political conflict — it is fundamentally a spiritual and intellectual battle. The forces arrayed against traditional values, religious liberty, and the natural family are motivated by ideologies that have their roots in bad philosophy: materialism, relativism, and a radical rejection of the created order.

To win this battle, conservatives must combine the best of faith and reason. Faith without reason becomes fideism — a blind belief disconnected from reality. Reason without faith becomes a truncated rationalism incapable of grounding human dignity, natural rights, or moral truth.

The good news is that faith and reason are natural allies, not enemies. The Christian intellectual tradition has always held that God created an intelligible universe and gave human beings the rational capacity to understand it. This is why the Christian West gave rise to universities, hospitals, and modern science itself.

The practical implications of this alliance are significant. On issues like gender ideology, the case against radical interventions on children's bodies is both scientific (the evidence for the "affirm only" model is weak) and moral (children cannot consent to irreversible procedures). On economic questions, both economic science and moral theology support the dignity of work and the importance of free exchange. On questions of life and family, reason and revelation speak with one voice.

The path forward requires courage, clarity, and the willingness to speak truth in a culture that increasingly punishes dissent. But the resources of the Christian intellectual tradition are more than adequate for the task.
