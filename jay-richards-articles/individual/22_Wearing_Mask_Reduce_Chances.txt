Title: Of Course Wearing a Mask Will Reduce Your Chances of Contracting COVID-19
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: 2020
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/of-course-wearing-a-mask-will-reduce-your-chances-of-contracting-covid-19/

In this article, Richards takes a position that might surprise readers familiar with his opposition to mask mandates. He argues that wearing a proper mask does indeed reduce the wearer's chances of contracting COVID-19 -- a point he distinguishes carefully from the question of whether governments should mandate masks for everyone.

Richards "never bought the advice from official groups against wearing masks." Early in the pandemic, public health authorities including the CDC and the WHO advised the general public against wearing masks, claiming they were unnecessary or even counterproductive for non-healthcare workers. Richards found this advice dubious from the start.

He and his wife wore N-95 masks they had purchased for household work before the pandemic. N-95 masks, unlike the cloth coverings that would later be mandated, actually filter airborne particles effectively. Richards saw the common-sense case for wearing them in high-risk settings long before official guidance caught up.

The article draws an important distinction between two separate questions: Does wearing a mask reduce your personal risk of infection? And should the government mandate that everyone wear a mask? Richards answers the first question with a clear yes, at least for proper masks worn in appropriate settings. But answering yes to the first question does not require answering yes to the second.

The case for personal mask-wearing in high-risk situations is straightforward. The case for a universal mandate is far more complicated, involving questions about the type of mask, the setting, the level of risk, individual variation, enforcement, and the proper limits of government authority. Richards argues that conflating these two questions -- as both pro-mandate and anti-mask partisans tend to do -- obscures rather than clarifies the issue.
