Title: 2022: The Year the Conspiracy Theory Became Conspiracy Fact
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: 2022
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/2022-the-year-the-conspiracy-theory-became-conspiracy-fact/

Richards reflects on the events of 2022 as a year in which numerous claims previously dismissed as "conspiracy theories" were vindicated by emerging evidence and official acknowledgments. The article examines how the label "conspiracy theory" has been used as a rhetorical weapon to suppress legitimate questions and dissenting viewpoints.

Throughout the COVID-19 pandemic and the broader political upheavals of recent years, many people who raised questions about official narratives -- regarding the origins of the virus, the efficacy of certain public health measures, the reliability of institutions, and other topics -- were dismissed as conspiracy theorists. Social media platforms censored their views. Mainstream media outlets ridiculed them. Professional consequences followed for those who spoke out.

Yet in 2022, Richards argues, many of these suppressed claims were confirmed or shown to be far more credible than the establishment had admitted. The article catalogs specific examples where the "conspiracy theory" label was applied to claims that turned out to be substantially true or at least well-founded.

Richards draws broader lessons from this pattern. The reflexive use of "conspiracy theory" as a dismissal is itself a form of intellectual dishonesty. It substitutes name-calling for argument and uses social pressure to enforce conformity. When institutions abuse the label to protect their preferred narratives rather than to identify genuinely irrational claims, they undermine public trust -- and rightly so.

The article calls for greater intellectual humility and openness to dissenting views, especially on matters of public policy where the stakes are high and the evidence is still developing. Richards argues that a healthy society needs robust debate, not enforced consensus, and that the willingness to entertain heterodox ideas is a sign of intellectual strength, not weakness.
