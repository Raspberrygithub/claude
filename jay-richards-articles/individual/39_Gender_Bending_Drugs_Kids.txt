Title: Giving Gender-Bending Drugs To Kids Is Worse Than We Thought
Authors: Jay W. Richards and Samuel Silvestro
Date: March 2023
Source: The Federalist
URL: https://thefederalist.com/2023/03/30/pumping-gender-bending-drugs-into-kids-is-even-more-dangerous-than-we-thought/

Co-authored with Samuel Silvestro, this article examines emerging evidence about the dangers of puberty blockers administered to children experiencing gender dysphoria. The authors argue that the risks of these drugs have been systematically understated by proponents of "gender-affirming care."

Puberty blockers -- primarily GnRH agonists -- are presented by gender medicine advocates as a safe, fully reversible "pause button" that gives children time to explore their gender identity. Richards and Silvestro challenge this characterization on multiple fronts, citing growing evidence of serious and potentially irreversible side effects.

The known risks include decreased bone density, potential impacts on brain development, effects on fertility, and emotional and psychological consequences. The article examines recent studies and clinical data suggesting that these risks are more severe and more common than earlier proponents acknowledged. Some children who were placed on puberty blockers have experienced lasting health consequences even after discontinuing the drugs.

The authors also challenge the claim that puberty blockers are "fully reversible." While it is true that natural puberty will resume if the drugs are stopped, the period of blocked development cannot be recovered. A child who spends several years on puberty blockers during a critical developmental window has missed experiences -- physical, neurological, and psychological -- that cannot simply be replayed later.

Furthermore, Richards and Silvestro point out that the vast majority of children placed on puberty blockers proceed to cross-sex hormones, suggesting that the drugs do not function as a neutral "pause" but rather as the first step on a pathway toward medical transition. This raises serious questions about informed consent, particularly for children and adolescents who may not fully understand the long-term implications of the choices being made on their behalf.

The article calls for greater caution, more rigorous research, and honest communication with parents and patients about the risks of puberty blockers.
