Title: Why Libertarians Shouldn't Be Atheists
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: March 2014
Source: Acton Blog
URL: https://blog.acton.org/archives/66648-libertarians-shouldnt-atheists.html

Richards challenges the common assumption that libertarianism and atheism are natural philosophical allies. The impression that atheism or materialism is an accomplished host for libertarian values is mistaken, he argues. In fact, the intellectual foundations of libertarianism are undermined, not supported, by an atheistic worldview.

Richards examines four areas that are lost by embracing an atheistic, materialistic worldview, each of which is essential to the libertarian project:

First, free will. Libertarianism depends on the reality of individual choice and personal responsibility. But a strictly materialistic worldview, in which everything is determined by prior physical causes, has no room for genuine free will. If human beings are nothing more than complex arrangements of matter governed by the laws of physics, then the concept of free choice -- and with it, the entire framework of individual rights and responsibilities -- becomes illusory.

Second, objective moral truth. Libertarians believe in natural rights -- rights that individuals possess by virtue of their humanity, not by grant of the state. But on a materialistic worldview, there are no objective moral truths. Morality becomes nothing more than personal preference or social convention, and "rights" become whatever the powerful say they are.

Third, human dignity. The libertarian commitment to individual liberty presupposes that human beings have inherent dignity and worth. But materialism provides no basis for this claim. If human beings are merely complex animals -- or, worse, merely complex machines -- then there is no principled reason to treat them as bearers of inalienable rights.

Fourth, rationality itself. Libertarian arguments depend on the reliability of human reason. But if our cognitive faculties are merely the product of blind evolutionary processes selected for survival rather than truth, there is no reason to trust them -- including when they produce libertarian conclusions.

The article links to Richards's full argument, "Why Libertarians Need God," which develops these points in greater detail.
