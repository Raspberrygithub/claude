Title: Herd Immunity To COVID Is Not Reckless. It Would Protect The Vulnerable
Authors: Jay W. Richards, Douglas Axe, and William Briggs
Date: October 2020
Source: The Federalist
URL: https://thefederalist.com/2020/10/19/herd-immunity-to-covid-is-not-reckless-it-would-protect-the-vulnerable/

Co-authored with Douglas Axe and William Briggs, this article defends the approach outlined in the Great Barrington Declaration -- a focused protection strategy that would shield the most vulnerable populations while allowing the broader population to resume normal life and build natural immunity to COVID-19.

The authors argue that the term "herd immunity" has been deliberately mischaracterized by proponents of lockdowns. Herd immunity is not a strategy to be adopted or rejected -- it is an epidemiological phenomenon that occurs naturally as a population develops immunity to an infectious disease, whether through infection or vaccination. The question is not whether herd immunity will eventually be reached but how it will be reached and at what cost.

The Great Barrington Declaration, authored by epidemiologists from Harvard, Stanford, and Oxford, proposed that the most humane and effective approach was focused protection: taking aggressive measures to shield the elderly and immunocompromised while allowing younger, healthier people to live relatively normal lives. This approach would minimize the collateral damage of lockdowns -- economic devastation, delayed medical care, mental health crises, and educational harm -- while still protecting those at greatest risk.

Critics labeled this approach "reckless" and accused its proponents of advocating mass death. Richards, Axe, and Briggs push back against these characterizations. The lockdown approach, they argue, was itself reckless -- it imposed enormous costs on the entire population, including the vulnerable, without demonstrating clear superiority in protecting the elderly. Many of the worst outbreaks occurred in nursing homes and long-term care facilities, where lockdowns did little to prevent transmission.

The article calls for an honest assessment of the tradeoffs involved in pandemic response and argues that focused protection, not universal lockdowns, represents the most ethical and effective approach to managing COVID-19.
