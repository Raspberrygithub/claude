Title: Why States Must Define Sex Precisely
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: March 2023
Source: The Heritage Foundation / Public Discourse
URL: https://www.heritage.org/gender/commentary/why-states-must-define-sex-precisely

Until recently, no precise legal definition of sex was needed because no one contested it. The biological distinction between male and female was so obvious and universally understood that lawmakers never felt compelled to spell it out in statute. Because of gender ideology's growing influence on laws and institutions, states no longer have this luxury.

Current efforts to redefine sex to include "gender identity" would dissolve sex as a stable legal category and create legal chaos. If sex is reduced to a subjective feeling rather than an objective biological reality, then every law, regulation, and policy built on the distinction between male and female becomes incoherent. Title IX protections for women in education, sex-segregated prisons, women's shelters, and sports categories all depend on a clear and stable definition of sex.

Any good definition will capture the central concept of biological sex -- the orientation of male and female bodies for reproduction. Males are organized around the production of small gametes (sperm), and females are organized around the production of large gametes (ova). This is a binary classification rooted in reproductive biology, not a spectrum or a social construct.

A sound legal definition will also refer to what happens under normal development while accounting for disorders. Disorders of sexual development (sometimes called "intersex" conditions) do not create a third sex or disprove the sex binary. They are medical conditions that affect the normal development of male or female bodies, just as other developmental disorders affect other bodily systems without creating new categories of human beings.

Public institutions must shore up their defenses by defining sex precisely in law. Without clear definitions, courts and bureaucrats will continue to reinterpret existing law through the lens of gender ideology, eroding sex-based protections and creating confusion. States that act now to define sex clearly in their legal codes will be better positioned to protect women's rights, children's welfare, and the integrity of their institutions against ideological capture.
