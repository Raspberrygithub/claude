Title: U.S. Military Uses Religious Test Against Service Members To Enforce Vaccine Mandate
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: January 2022
Source: The Federalist
URL: https://thefederalist.com/2022/01/03/u-s-military-uses-religious-test-against-service-members-to-enforce-vaccine-mandate/

Richards examines how the U.S. military's COVID-19 vaccine mandate effectively imposed a religious test on service members who sought religious exemptions. The article argues that the military's process for evaluating religious exemption requests was designed not to accommodate sincere religious beliefs but to deny as many requests as possible.

Service members who filed religious exemption requests were subjected to interrogation about the sincerity and consistency of their religious beliefs. Chaplains and commanding officers were tasked with evaluating whether a service member's religious objection was genuine -- a process that raises serious constitutional concerns under the First Amendment's prohibition of religious tests for government service.

Richards documents cases in which service members were asked intrusive questions about their religious practices, challenged on the consistency of their beliefs, and denied exemptions on pretextual grounds. The approval rate for religious exemptions was vanishingly small, suggesting that the process was designed to produce denials rather than to genuinely accommodate religious liberty.

The article argues that this approach violates both the letter and spirit of religious liberty protections. The government is not competent to evaluate the sincerity of religious beliefs, and the attempt to do so inevitably involves the kind of religious test that the Constitution prohibits. When the military denies a religious exemption on the grounds that the service member's beliefs are insufficiently consistent or sincere, it is making a theological judgment that lies outside its authority.

Richards calls for robust protection of religious liberty within the military and warns that the precedent set by the vaccine mandate could be used to override religious convictions on other issues in the future.
