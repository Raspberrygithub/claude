Title: Authentic Intelligence is the New AI
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: Undated
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/authentic-intelligence-new-ai/

This article serves as a review and promotion of Richards's book The Human Advantage, which argues that the rise of artificial intelligence makes authentic human intelligence more important, not less. Richards plays on the acronym "AI" to argue that in an age of artificial intelligence, what the world most needs is authentic intelligence -- the distinctly human capacities that no machine can replicate.

The book and article address widespread anxieties about automation and artificial intelligence replacing human workers. Richards acknowledges that AI will transform the economy and displace certain kinds of work, but argues that the most important human capacities -- creativity, moral reasoning, empathy, leadership, and the ability to find meaning -- are precisely the qualities that machines cannot replicate.

Richards challenges the materialistic assumption that underlies much AI hype: the idea that human intelligence is merely computation, and that sufficiently powerful computers will therefore replicate or surpass human thought. This assumption, he argues, reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of what human intelligence is. Authentic intelligence involves not just processing information but understanding meaning, making moral judgments, forming relationships, and pursuing purpose -- capacities that are categorically different from what any computer does.

The article argues that the proper response to the rise of AI is not fear but a renewed appreciation for what makes human beings unique. Rather than competing with machines on their own terms -- speed, efficiency, data processing -- human beings should cultivate the authentically human qualities that will be most valuable in an increasingly automated world.

Richards offers practical advice for individuals seeking to thrive in the age of AI: develop skills that require genuine human judgment, cultivate relationships and emotional intelligence, pursue work that involves creativity and moral reasoning, and resist the temptation to reduce yourself to a biological machine competing with silicon ones.
