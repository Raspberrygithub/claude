Title: Why States Must Define Sex Precisely
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: March 2023
Source: Public Discourse
URL: https://www.thepublicdiscourse.com/2023/03/88194/

This Public Discourse version of Richards's argument for precise legal definitions of sex was published in the Witherspoon Institute's online journal. The article makes the case that the growing influence of gender ideology on American law and institutions requires states to adopt clear, biologically grounded definitions of sex in their legal codes.

Until recently, no precise legal definition of sex was needed because no one contested the basic biological distinction between male and female. But gender ideology has introduced the concept of "gender identity" as a replacement for or supplement to biological sex in legal frameworks, creating confusion and threatening sex-based protections.

Richards argues that any sound legal definition of sex must be grounded in reproductive biology. Sex is the orientation of male and female bodies for reproduction -- males produce small gametes, females produce large gametes. This binary classification encompasses the entire human species and is not undermined by the existence of disorders of sexual development, which are medical conditions affecting the normal development of male or female bodies.

The article engages with the philosophical and legal arguments for incorporating "gender identity" into sex definitions and finds them wanting. Gender identity is a subjective, self-reported psychological state that cannot be objectively verified. Basing legal categories on such a foundation would dissolve sex as a stable legal concept and create cascading problems for every area of law that depends on the male-female distinction.

Richards provides guidance for lawmakers on how to craft definitions that are both scientifically accurate and legally workable. A good definition must capture the central concept, account for edge cases without abandoning the binary framework, and be robust enough to withstand legal challenge. The article offers specific language and principles that state legislators can use as they work to protect sex-based legal categories from ideological redefinition.
