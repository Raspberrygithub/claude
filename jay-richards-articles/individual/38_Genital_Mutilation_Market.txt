Title: In The Genital Mutilation Market, Business Is Booming
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: October 2022
Source: The Federalist
URL: https://thefederalist.com/2022/10/14/in-the-sex-reassignment-surgery-market-business-is-booming/

Richards examines the rapid growth of the sex reassignment surgery market and the financial incentives driving the expansion of gender medicine. The article argues that the dramatic increase in gender transition procedures -- particularly among minors and young adults -- is being fueled in part by the profit motives of hospitals, surgeons, pharmaceutical companies, and clinics.

The gender medicine industry has grown from a niche field serving a small number of adult patients into a booming market targeting an ever-expanding population. The number of gender clinics has multiplied, pharmaceutical companies profit from lifelong hormone prescriptions, and surgeons perform increasingly radical procedures on younger and younger patients. The financial incentives all point in one direction: more diagnoses, more treatments, and more surgeries.

Richards argues that this financial dynamic distorts medical judgment. When a hospital stands to earn hundreds of thousands of dollars from a single patient's course of gender transition -- puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones, and multiple surgeries -- there is an inherent conflict of interest in the diagnostic process. The standard of care in gender medicine has shifted from careful, cautious evaluation to rapid affirmation, and the financial incentives help explain why.

The article also examines the marketing and promotional strategies used by gender clinics to attract patients. Some clinics actively recruit through social media, school presentations, and partnerships with activist organizations. The language of "gender-affirming care" frames invasive and irreversible procedures as medically necessary and compassionate, making it difficult for patients, parents, or insurers to push back.

Richards draws parallels to other instances in medical history where financial incentives led to overdiagnosis and overtreatment, with devastating consequences for patients. He calls for greater scrutiny of the gender medicine industry and its financial incentives, arguing that the health of vulnerable patients must take priority over the bottom line.
