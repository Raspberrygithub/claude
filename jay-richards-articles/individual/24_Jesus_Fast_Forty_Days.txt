Title: Why Did Jesus Fast for Forty Days and Forty Nights? And Should We?
Author: Jay W. Richards
Date: Undated
Source: The Stream
URL: https://stream.org/jesus-fast-forty-days-forty-nights/

Richards explores the biblical account of Jesus's 40-day fast in the wilderness and its implications for contemporary Christian practice. The Gospel accounts describe Jesus retreating into the desert after his baptism, where he fasted for 40 days and 40 nights before being tempted by the devil.

The number 40 carries deep significance in biblical tradition. Moses spent 40 days on Mount Sinai. The Israelites wandered 40 years in the wilderness. Elijah fasted 40 days on his journey to Mount Horeb. Jesus's 40-day fast echoes and fulfills these earlier episodes, connecting his ministry to the great narrative arc of salvation history.

Richards examines what Jesus's fast teaches about the nature and purpose of fasting itself. Fasting is not merely an ascetic exercise or a demonstration of willpower. It is a spiritual discipline that subordinates the desires of the body to the needs of the soul. By fasting, the believer acknowledges that human beings do not live by bread alone -- that there are realities more fundamental than physical comfort and material satisfaction.

The article considers whether and how contemporary Christians should emulate Jesus's example. Richards does not suggest that every Christian should attempt a literal 40-day fast, which would be physically dangerous for most people without careful preparation. Instead, he argues that Jesus's fast points toward the importance of incorporating regular fasting into the Christian life -- whether through the traditional fasting days and seasons of the liturgical calendar, through periodic extended fasts, or through other forms of voluntary self-denial.

Richards connects the practice of fasting to the broader themes of spiritual warfare, self-discipline, and dependence on God that run throughout the Christian tradition.
