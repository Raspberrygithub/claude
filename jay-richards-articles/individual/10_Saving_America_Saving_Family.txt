Title: Saving America by Saving the Family
Authors: Jay W. Richards, Roger Severino, Emma Waters, Delano Squires, Rachel Sheffield, and Robert Rector
Date: January 2026
Source: The Heritage Foundation
URL: https://www.heritage.org/marriage-and-family/report/saving-america-saving-the-family-foundation-the-next-250-years

This Heritage Foundation report, co-authored by Jay W. Richards along with Roger Severino, Emma Waters, Delano Squires, Rachel Sheffield, and Robert Rector, argues that the family is the foundational institution of American civilization and that its restoration is essential to the nation's future.

As America approaches its 250th anniversary, the authors contend that the health of the republic depends on the health of the family. The family is the first and most important institution of civil society -- the place where children are raised, values are transmitted, and citizens are formed. When families break down, the consequences ripple outward through every other institution: schools, churches, communities, and government.

The report examines the current state of the American family, documenting trends in marriage rates, divorce, out-of-wedlock births, and family structure. It presents evidence that children raised in stable, married, two-parent households fare significantly better on virtually every measure of well-being -- educational achievement, economic mobility, physical and mental health, and avoidance of crime and substance abuse.

The authors argue that public policy has too often undermined the family through perverse incentives in welfare programs, tax policy, and cultural messaging. They propose a comprehensive agenda for strengthening the family, including reforms to marriage penalties in the tax code and welfare system, support for parental rights in education, and policies that recognize and reinforce the unique importance of the married, two-parent family.

The report positions family restoration not as a nostalgic project but as a forward-looking strategy essential to securing America's future for the next 250 years. Without strong families, the authors argue, no amount of government spending or institutional reform can sustain a free and prosperous society.
