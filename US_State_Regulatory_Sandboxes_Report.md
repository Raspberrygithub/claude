# U.S. State Regulatory Sandboxes: A Comprehensive Report
## Analysis of Regulatory Innovation Programs Across American States (2005-2025)

---

## Executive Summary

Over the past two decades, U.S. states have increasingly adopted regulatory sandbox programs to foster innovation while maintaining consumer protections. These programs allow businesses to test innovative products and services with temporary waivers from certain regulatory requirements. As of early 2025, at least **18 states** have enacted some form of regulatory sandbox legislation, covering fintech, insurance, legal services, healthcare technology, and general/universal applications.

**Key Findings:**
- Arizona pioneered the first U.S. fintech regulatory sandbox in 2018
- Utah created the nation's first universal (all-industry) regulatory sandbox in 2021
- Most state sandboxes have struggled to attract participants, with notable exceptions being Arizona, Hawaii, and Utah's legal services sandbox
- Five states now have universal sandboxes: Utah, Kentucky, West Virginia, Missouri, and Kansas

## State-by-State Analysis

### 1. ARIZONA
**Established:** 2018 (First in the nation)
**Legislation:** House Bill 2434
**Administering Agency:** Arizona Attorney General's Office
**Status:** Active

#### Scope
- **Focus Area:** Financial Technology (Fintech)
- **Covered Activities:** Money transmission, consumer lending, investment advice, payment processing, cryptocurrency/blockchain
- **Duration:** Up to 2 years with possible 1-year extension
- **Consumer Cap:** 10,000 customers (expandable to 17,500)

#### Participants & Results
- First participant: **Omni Mobile Inc.** (mobile payment platform)
- Other participants include:
  - Align Income Share Funding, Inc. (income share agreements)
  - ENIAN, Ltd.
  - Verdigris Holdings Inc.
  - GiveCorporation Inc. (merchant services with fraud detection) - joined March 2023
- **Total participants since inception:** ~13 companies
- **Currently active:** 3 companies
- Products tested include cryptocurrency payments for cannabis businesses, algorithms for renewable energy project evaluation, and cash-for-future-earnings models

#### Success Assessment
**Rating: Moderate Success**
- Arizona remains the most successful state fintech sandbox by participation numbers
- Allows non-resident companies to participate (key differentiator)
- Has reciprocity agreements with other jurisdictions including Utah, UK, and Singapore
- Notable departure: Shane Foster, who co-led the sandbox, joined Greenberg Traurig law firm in January 2024

### 2. UTAH

#### A. General Regulatory Sandbox
**Established:** 2021 (First universal sandbox in the nation)
**Legislation:** Various bills creating Office of Regulatory Relief
**Administering Agency:** Utah Office of Regulatory Relief (under Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity)
**Status:** Active

##### Scope
- **Focus Area:** Universal/All Industries
- **Covered Activities:** Any innovative product, service, or business model across all sectors
- **Duration:** 1 year initial period with annual extensions available

##### Participants & Results
- **Homie Title:** Saved customers over $13 million since sandbox admission
- **TowPro:** Successfully executed over 650 emergency, non-consent tows in northern Utah municipalities

##### Success Assessment
**Rating: Successful**
- First-of-its-kind all-inclusive regulatory sandbox
- Model for other states including Kansas and Missouri

#### B. Fintech Regulatory Sandbox
**Established:** 2019
**Status:** Active (merged into general sandbox framework)

#### C. Legal Services Sandbox
**Established:** 2020
**Administering Agency:** Utah Supreme Court/Office of Legal Services Innovation
**Status:** Active (with significant changes)

##### Scope
- **Focus Area:** Legal services innovation
- **Covered Activities:** Nonlawyer legal service providers, alternative business structures, AI-assisted legal tools
- **Target Population:** Underserved consumers seeking legal assistance

##### Participants & Results
- **Peak participation:** 39 entities (2022)
- **Current participation:** 11 entities (April 2025)
- **Services delivered:** 75,000+ legal services to ~24,000 unique consumers (as of January 2024)
- **Consumer complaints:** Only 20 total (harm-to-service ratio of 1:5,869)
- **Harm complaints:** 9 complaints determined to raise potential consumer harm (ratio of 1:8,468 services)

##### Success Assessment
**Rating: Initially Successful, Now Constrained**
- Four entities specifically target free legal services to low-income individuals
- Utah Supreme Court enacted "Phase 2" requirements in September 2024, significantly narrowing scope
- Program authorized through August 2027
- Outcomes Evaluation expected November 2025

### 5. KENTUCKY

#### A. Insurance Regulatory Sandbox
**Established:** 2019 (First comprehensive insurtech sandbox in the nation)
**Legislation:** House Bill 386
**Administering Agency:** Kentucky Department of Insurance
**Status:** Active (extended through 2030)

##### Scope
- **Focus Area:** Insurance Technology (Insurtech)
- **Covered Activities:** Innovative insurance products and services
- **Requirements:**
  - $750 filing fee
  - Minimum $25,000 available funds
  - Detailed technical and business plan
  - Consumer protection demonstrations

##### Participants & Results
- **As of November 2021:** No participants accepted

##### Success Assessment
**Rating: Unsuccessful to Date**
- Recent extension to 2030 suggests continued state commitment
- Stringent entry requirements may be deterring applicants

#### B. General Regulatory Sandbox
**Established:** 2022 (HB 264 effective March 15, 2024)
**Administering Agency:** Kentucky Office of Regulatory Relief (under Attorney General's Office)
**Status:** Active

##### Scope
- **Focus Area:** Universal/All Industries
- **Covered Activities:** Any innovative product, production method, or service
- **Structure:** Advisory committee makes recommendations; Director appointed by Attorney General

##### Success Assessment
**Rating: Too Early to Assess**
- Passed with overwhelming bipartisan support (96-0 in House)
- Framework allows successful demonstrations to inform regulatory reform recommendations

### 7. HAWAII
**Established:** 2020
**Program Name:** Digital Currency Innovation Lab (DCIL)
**Administering Agency:** Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs
**Status:** **Concluded** (June 30, 2024)

#### Scope
- **Focus Area:** Digital Currency/Cryptocurrency
- **Covered Activities:** Cryptocurrency exchange, digital asset services

#### Participants & Results
- **Total participants:** 11 companies
- **Notable participants:**
  - Facebook's Novi Financial
  - Gemini Trust
  - Robinhood
  - Cloud Nalu (Hawaii-based)

#### Final Outcomes
- Program concluded that digital currency transactions are NOT best regulated through existing money transmitter laws
- Cryptocurrency companies will no longer require Hawaii money transmitter license
- Companies may operate as unregulated businesses at state level (must still comply with federal requirements)
- Hawaii joined Wyoming in explicitly not regulating cryptocurrency under money transmission licensing regimes

#### Success Assessment
**Rating: Successful (as pilot research program)**
- Achieved its research objectives
- Led to regulatory clarity for crypto businesses
- Provided data-driven policy decisions

### 9. NORTH CAROLINA
**Established:** 2021
**Legislation:** HB 624 (Regulatory Sandbox Act)
**Administering Agency:** North Carolina Innovation Council
**Status:** Active (officially launched August 2024)

#### Scope
- **Focus Area:** Fintech, Insurtech, and Emerging Technologies
- **Covered Activities:** AI, blockchain, data privacy solutions in regulated financial and insurance markets
- **Duration:** 24 months
- **Unique Feature:** No statutory end date (unlike many other state sandboxes)

#### Structure
- Innovation Council: 11 members including Commissioner of Banks, Commissioner of Insurance, Secretary of State, Attorney General, and 7 public members
- Executive Director appointed August 2024
- Permanent rules effective March 1, 2025

#### Participants & Results
- Applications opened Fall 2024
- No participant data available yet (program newly launched)

#### Success Assessment
**Rating: Too Early to Assess**
- Comprehensive structure with multi-agency involvement
- Long development period (2021-2024) may indicate careful planning

## Key Success Factors Analysis

### What Makes Sandboxes Successful

1. **Flexible Residency Requirements**
   - Arizona and Hawaii allow non-resident companies
   - Wyoming requires physical presence → struggled to attract participants

2. **Reciprocity Provisions**
   - States with reciprocity (Arizona, Utah, Wyoming, Nevada) offer cross-border benefits
   - 11-state multistate licensing agreement enhances attractiveness

3. **Adequate Staffing**
   - Arizona and West Virginia operated with <1 FTE
   - Recommendation: More staff and legal expertise improves outcomes

4. **Clear Value Proposition**
   - Hawaii's program had defined research goals → achieved clear outcomes
   - Programs without clear differentiation from traditional licensing struggle

5. **Universal vs. Industry-Specific**
   - Universal sandboxes (Utah, Missouri, Kansas) offer broader appeal
   - Industry-specific sandboxes may limit potential participant pool

### Common Challenges

1. **Low Participation Rates**
   - Most U.S. sandbox programs have been unsuccessful in attracting participants
   - Overly strict entry criteria are a common problem

2. **Rural State Disadvantage**
   - Wyoming, West Virginia, South Dakota, Kentucky face geographic challenges
   - Less population density = smaller potential participant pool

3. **Regulatory Uncertainty**
   - Companies may prefer traditional licensing for certainty
   - Sandbox duration limits (2-3 years) create business planning challenges

4. **Limited Consumer Base**
   - Consumer caps (5,000-17,500) restrict meaningful market testing
   - Insufficient scale for venture capital validation

## Global Context & Comparative Analysis

### International Sandbox Performance

For comparison, research on international regulatory sandboxes shows:

- **UK Financial Conduct Authority Sandbox (2016):**
  - 15% increase in capital raised post-entry
  - 50% higher probability of raising capital
  - 80% of tested firms still operating
  - 15% higher likelihood of securing subsequent funding
  - 6.6x more fintech investment than peers

- **Global Adoption:**
  - 160+ sandboxes in 80+ countries since 2016
  - Southeast Asia (Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand) particularly successful

### U.S. vs. International Performance

U.S. state sandboxes have generally underperformed compared to:
- UK's FCA sandbox (hundreds of participants)
- Singapore's regulatory sandbox (cross-border experimentation)
- Australia's ASIC sandbox

**Contributing factors:**
- Fragmented state-by-state approach vs. national frameworks
- Lack of federal sandbox creates regulatory arbitrage
- State population sizes limit market testing value

## Federal Developments

### CFPB Initiatives (2025)
- New No-Action Letter (NAL) and Compliance Assistance Sandbox (CAST) policies
- Focus on "new financial products or services" (not minor adjustments)
- Novel approach: extends approvals to applicant's competitors

### Federal Sandbox Legislation
- No comprehensive federal fintech sandbox exists
- Various proposals have been introduced but not enacted
- State programs remain primary U.S. sandbox mechanism

## Recommendations for Policymakers

### For States Considering Sandboxes

1. **Study Arizona and Hawaii models** - both achieved meaningful participation
2. **Consider universal scope** - following Utah, Missouri, Kansas examples
3. **Allow non-resident participation** - critical for attracting companies
4. **Establish reciprocity** - leverage multistate agreements
5. **Adequately staff programs** - invest in legal and administrative expertise
6. **Set clear success metrics** - define what "success" means upfront

### For Existing Sandbox Programs

1. **Review entry criteria** - overly strict requirements deter participation
2. **Increase consumer caps** - current limits may be insufficient for meaningful testing
3. **Extend duration options** - 2 years may be insufficient for some innovations
4. **Document and share outcomes** - transparency builds ecosystem confidence

---

## Conclusion

U.S. state regulatory sandboxes represent a significant experiment in regulatory innovation. While the concept has shown promise internationally, American implementation has yielded mixed results. Arizona and Utah's legal services sandbox stand out as relative successes, while most state programs have struggled to attract participants.

The trend toward universal sandboxes (Utah, Kentucky, West Virginia, Missouri, Kansas) represents an evolution from narrow industry-specific programs. This approach may prove more successful as it offers broader applicability and greater flexibility.

Key indicators to watch:
- Whether newly launched programs (North Carolina, Ohio, Missouri, Kansas, Louisiana) achieve better participation
- Impact of federal sandbox developments on state programs
- Continued evolution of Utah's legal services sandbox under "Phase 2" restrictions
- Long-term outcomes of Hawaii's regulatory determination on cryptocurrency

The regulatory sandbox concept remains promising, but U.S. states must continue refining their approaches to achieve meaningful innovation benefits while protecting consumers.
